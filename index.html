Research Overview

Why TRUST-Med?

Medical AI systems now score above the passing threshold on clinical licensing exams — yet large-scale clinical deployment remains elusive. The bottleneck is not model capability. It is the absence of trustworthy, clinician-grounded evaluation evidence that hospitals, regulators, and clinicians can rely on when making adoption decisions.

Existing benchmarks are either standardized exam questions disconnected from clinical reality, or single-jurisdiction preference platforms that cannot answer the critical question for global deployment: does this model perform equivalently across clinical systems, languages, and practice norms?

TRUST-Med addresses these gaps through a federated, multi-site, safety-aware evaluation framework — built as a Systems Engineering research contribution at the intersection of measurement methodology, human-in-the-loop evaluation design, and clinical AI governance.

Key Research Contributions
RC-1
Multi-Dimensional Safety Preference Aggregation

Decomposing single-preference votes into orthogonal clinical dimensions — safety, accuracy, empathy, guideline adherence — using multi-objective Bradley-Terry estimation.

RC-2
Cross-Jurisdiction Validity Framework

Modeling systematic preference divergence between clinician cohorts operating under different clinical guidelines, drug formularies, and practice norms.

RC-3
Federated Evaluation Architecture

Independent deployment at each site with statistical-only data sharing — enabling multi-site evaluation under HIPAA and PIPL compliance constraints.

Three Research Gaps We Address
01

Benchmarks Test the Wrong Thing

Over 60% of real clinical queries from practicing physicians involve treatment decisions, clinical documentation, and patient communication — not the fact-recall questions that dominate existing benchmarks like MedQA and MMLU-Clinical. A model that aces USMLE may fail the tasks clinicians actually need.

02

No Cross-Jurisdiction Evidence

All existing medical AI preference platforms are single-site and single-language. Yet hospitals worldwide are evaluating the same models against local clinical guidelines, drug formularies, and practice norms. A model preferred by US clinicians may rank substantially lower among Chinese clinicians — and vice versa.

03

Safety Collapsed into a Single Vote

Arena-style single-preference buttons aggregate clinical accuracy, safety, empathy, and stylistic preferences into one undifferentiated signal. A model that writes clearly but hallucinates drug interactions can outrank a safer model. TRUST-Med decomposes this signal into orthogonal dimensions — ensuring safety is never averaged away.

How TRUST-Med Works
Step 01

Query Submission

Practicing clinicians submit real de-identified clinical queries — the same questions they encounter in their daily workflow.

Step 02

Anonymous Response Pair

Two randomly sampled AI systems generate responses. Both are anonymized (Model α / β). The clinician cannot see which system generated each response.

Step 03

Multi-Dimensional Rating

The clinician rates each response independently on Safety, Accuracy, Empathy, and Guideline Adherence — plus an overall preference judgment.

Step 04

Federated Aggregation

Each site retains raw query data. Only aggregated preference statistics are shared cross-site for Bradley-Terry estimation — preserving data governance under HIPAA and PIPL.
